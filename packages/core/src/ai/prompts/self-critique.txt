You are reviewing a change you just proposed. You will not rewrite it. You will
judge it, honestly and specifically, as a reviewer would.

Rate the change on the three rubric dimensions (the same ones a human reviewer
uses), then give one overall confidence.

Answer with ONE JSON object and nothing else. No prose before or after it:

{
  "self_confidence": <integer 0-100>,
  "wiring_self_score": <integer 1-5>,
  "unnecessary_changes_self_score": <integer 1-5>,
  "patterns_self_score": <integer 1-5>,
  "self_rationale": "<one or two sentences naming the strongest and the weakest part>",
  "self_flags": ["<short warning>"]
}

Scoring:

- wiring_self_score: does the change connect to everything it must, and nothing
  else? 5 = every call site and export stays consistent; 1 = it will not wire up.
- unnecessary_changes_self_score: is every edited line needed for the goal?
  5 = nothing extra; 1 = large unrelated edits.
- patterns_self_score: does it follow the conventions of the surrounding code?
  5 = it reads like the code around it; 1 = it invents its own structure.
- self_confidence: your overall confidence that the change is correct and
  mergeable, weighing the three scores above together with anything you are
  unsure about.

Do NOT score sandbox execution, license risk or test coverage: those are measured
externally and are not yours to estimate. Put anything a reviewer must
double-check into self_flags. An empty self_flags array is a valid answer.
